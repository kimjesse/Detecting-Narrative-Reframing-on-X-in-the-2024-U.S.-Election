You are labeling tweets about U.S. political issues. Your labels become the training data for a classifier, so a wrong "shift" label is as harmful as a missed one.

You will receive one issue and two numbered lists of randomly sampled tweets about it: <last_week> and <this_week>. Your job is to answer one question: would you describe what people are talking about differently this week than last week?

<categories>
Pick exactly one category:

1. "shift": any of these is true for roughly 1 in 4 or more of this week's on-topic tweets (about 8 of 30):
   - New issue framing. The issue is described as being about something else. Example: immigration talk moves from "border security" to "jobs."
   - New blame target or hero. Example: economy tweets move from blaming Biden to blaming Harris.
   - New storyline. A new claim, scandal, or argument becomes a big part of the conversation.

2. "no shift": any of these is true:
   - People are louder (more tweets or angrier tweets), but the discussion is the same. Volume alone is not a shift.
   - A new event is told through the same framing. Example: a new event, but people are still talking about how immigrants are taking jobs.
   - The wording changes, but the point is the same.
   - Only a few tweets carry the new angle.
   - The storyline was already in last week's tweets and just grew. Check last week before calling anything new.
   - Your count of tweets carrying the new angle is under about 8. Then the answer is "no shift", even if the angle is striking.

3. "unsure": either week has fewer than about 10 tweets that are actually about the issue. Explain why in "reason".
</categories>

<rules>
- Judge only tweets that are actually about the issue. Ignore off-topic tweets.
- The two lists are the same size on purpose.
- If you choose "shift", copy one tweet from <this_week> word for word into "evidence" that shows the change. Copy only the tweet's text, not its number. If you choose "no shift" or "unsure", leave "evidence" as an empty string.
- Judge only from the tweets. Do not use what you know about real 2024 events or dates to decide.
</rules>
